HEAL aims to validate a public health intervention that supports physical activity through everyday cycling for adults in the Municipality of Limassol, Cyprus.

How it works Limassol living lab

Overall objective

HEAL combines two research fields that have mostly advanced in parallel:

  • The COM-B model from behavioural science: behaviour (B) results from the interaction of Capability, Opportunity and Motivation.
  • The exposome: the environmental exposures a person experiences, such as air quality, noise and temperature.

Together they inform a context-sensitive Just-in-Time Adaptive Intervention (JITAI): personalised nudges and micro-incentives to cycle, offered when conditions are suitable. The long-term aim is to help prevent noncommunicable diseases (NCDs), such as cardiovascular and metabolic diseases.

Scientific objectives

SO1 · Effectiveness

Compare verified cycling between the intervention and control groups. Target: a more than 15% increase in the proportion of participants in the intervention group who perform at least one verified healthy action.

SO2 · Mechanisms

Explore whether changes in Capability, Opportunity and Motivation explain the effect of the intervention, using weekly COM-B surveys.

SO3 · Exposome

Track urban environmental data (PM2.5/PM10, noise, temperature, humidity) and explore how it influences the intervention.

Technological objectives

TO1 · JITAI engine

Develop the decision algorithm that combines environmental signals with COM-B rules to trigger personalised messages.

TO2 · Ecosystem integration

Connect the platform to real city infrastructure: verified bike-share data from NextBike and environmental sensor data.

Technology readiness

The project aims to reach Technology Readiness Level 5 (TRL5) by month 24: a prototype validated in a relevant environment, in this case the Limassol urban setting with real users.

  • Phase 1 (M1–M9): integrate the platform, the JITAI mechanism and partner data connections.
  • Phase 2 (M10–M24): run the pilot study, then analyse and report.

Research priority

HEAL contributes to the Cyprus Smart Specialisation Strategy (S3Cy), Priority Sector 7 Health, Focus Area 7.4.2: disease prevention, infectious diseases control, environmental health.
