What is the study?

HEAL is a small pilot study that aims to find out whether personalised, well-timed support can help adults in Limassol cycle more as part of daily life. It is a feasibility study: it tests how the approach works in practice so that a larger study can be planned.

Who can take part?

Adults who live and work in the Municipality of Limassol. The study will include 20 participants. Full eligibility criteria will be published here when recruitment opens.

What would I be asked to do?

  1. Consent and baseline. Give informed consent, complete a short survey and some basic health measurements (for example blood pressure).
  2. Random group allocation. Half of the participants join the intervention group and half the control group, by chance.
  3. Six months of everyday life. The intervention group receives occasional messages and small rewards for verified rides on NextBike public bikes. The control group receives standard health information.
  4. Weekly check-ins. Short weekly surveys about your capability, opportunity and motivation to cycle.

How is my data protected?

  • The project follows the GDPR, with a Data Protection Officer at CUT.
  • Personal identifiers are stored separately and encrypted on CUT servers in the EU.
  • Research data is pseudonymised before analysis; any shared dataset is fully anonymised.
  • You can withdraw at any time, and your data will be removed from active analyses.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need my own bicycle?

No. The study uses the NextBike public bike-sharing system in Limassol.

Do I need a special app?

The study communicates through WhatsApp or SMS. Details will be confirmed when recruitment opens.

How long does the study last?

The active part of the study lasts six months.

Can I stop taking part?

Yes, at any time and without giving a reason.
