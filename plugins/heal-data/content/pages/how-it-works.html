HEAL is designed as a closed loop: the right support at the right time, and every verified ride feeds back into the system.

  1. When

    The exposome sets the moment

    Environmental data such as air quality, temperature and noise help decide when a suggestion to cycle is useful and safe. No prompt to ride in a storm or in poor air.

  2. How

    A just-in-time nudge

    The Just-in-Time Adaptive Intervention (JITAI) sends a short, personalised message by WhatsApp or SMS, informed by the participant's Capability, Opportunity and Motivation (COM-B).

  3. Why

    A micro-incentive

    Small rewards aim to make the healthy choice the easy and attractive one, for example at the usual time of the commute to and from work.

  4. Data

    A verified ride comes back

    A ride on a NextBike public bike is verified automatically through the partner's data connection. It becomes data that helps refine the next suggestion.

An example

"The temperature is pleasant and a NextBike bicycle is 5 minutes away. A 15-minute ride on the green route now earns you points."

The aim is support that feels like a natural, helpful choice rather than a digital interruption.

Why it is different

  • Theory and context together. HEAL aims to be the first pilot to combine the COM-B model with the exposome in one intervention.
  • Explaining how it works. Weekly COM-B surveys aim to show why behaviour changes, not only whether it does.
  • Built on what the city already has. The intervention uses the existing NextBike public bike-sharing system and local partners.
